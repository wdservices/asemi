Can Expired Skincare or Medicine Be Relabeled? How to Spot a Fake Expiry Date

Nov 14, 2026·5 min read

Unscrupulous resellers routinely scrub faded ink off unsold inventory and stamp fresh dates on top. Here is how to spot tampered expiration marks and confirm factory dates digitally.

Key takeaways

  • Relabeling expired stock is common in clearance supply chains for sunscreens, active serums, infant formula, and over-the-counter packs.
  • Peelable date stickers, smudged acetone rings around the stamp, and mismatched outer-box dates are clear warning signs.
  • Locking manufacture and expiry dates in Asemi lets buyers verify real factory shelf-life independently of outer ink.

The quiet danger of wiped and restamped expiration dates

Every factory produces batches with strict shelf lives. Active skincare ingredients like retinol, sunscreen filters, and vitamin C degrade after expiration, while degraded medicines lose potency or turn harmful.

Instead of writing off expired cartons, illicit middlemen buy liquidated or expired stock cheaply, wipe off original ink with solvents, and stamp fresh dates—or stick new adhesive labels directly over old boxes.

How to spot a relabeled or altered expiry date

Genuine factories print dates inline using laser etching, crimped tube embossing, or crisp dot-matrix inkjet codes integrated right alongside the batch number.

  1. 01Tilt the box or bottle under bright light near the expiry date: matte scuff marks, glossy smudges, or faded plastic often betray solvent wiping.
  2. 02Feel whether the expiry date sits on a raised adhesive sticker placed over an older printed box area.
  3. 03Check that the date format matches the brand's country standard and aligns logically with the batch number.
  4. 04Verify whether the tube crimp indentation shows doubled or overwritten digits.
