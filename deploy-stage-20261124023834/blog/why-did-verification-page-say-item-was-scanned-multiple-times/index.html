Why Did a Verification Page Say My Item Was Scanned Multiple Times?

Nov 11, 2026·4 min read

Seeing 'Verified original, checked multiple times' can feel alarming if you are not expecting it. Here is how to interpret scan counts and check packaging seals calmly.

Key takeaways

  • A scan count of 2 or 3 often happens if you refreshed the page, scanned once in store and once at home, or checked with two phones.
  • Dozens of scans across different sessions strongly indicate that a counterfeiter photographed one genuine label and mass-printed it.
  • Asemi confirms when the underlying serial is original first, then guides you calmly on checking seals and reporting clones.

Normal repeat scans vs. cloned packaging warnings

The first time you verify a brand-new item out of a sealed carton, the scan counter starts at 1. If you bookmark the tab, reopen your browser later that week, or let your partner check the same bottle on their phone, the counter climbs naturally to 2 or 3.

Trouble begins when a fraud ring buys a single original jar or garment, scans its QR code, and reprints that exact sticker across 2,000 counterfeit boxes. Suddenly, shoppers across Lagos, London, and Nairobi all scan the very same code—driving the counter upward across unrelated sessions.

What to inspect when you see a multi-scan notice

Seeing a repeat-scan notice does not automatically mean your item is fake—especially if the count is low and you scanned it before. Run a quick sanity check before deciding whether to open or return the item.

  1. 01Consider whether you, a store clerk, or a household member already scanned the visible outer label before purchase.
  2. 02Check the scan count: 2–3 scans on an outer store-facing box differs dramatically from 25+ scans across different buyers.
  3. 03Inspect tamper-evident seals, inner foil liners, and shrink wrap for signs of prior opening or sloppy reapplication.
  4. 04Use Asemi's one-click report button if the item came sealed yet already displays high scan counts from strangers.
